## AI PROTOCOL 2030–2040: Protocol for Society under Accelerated Intelligence

The decisive question of the 2030–2040 period will not be whether artificial intelligence becomes more capable. The decisive question will be whether human beings, institutions and the international system establish legitimate rules quickly enough to govern that capability.

AI PROTOCOL therefore treats the next decade not as a prediction exercise, but as a governance horizon. Specific technologies may change. The underlying requirements of legitimacy, dignity, accountability, provenance, human review and institutional responsibility must not.

> The protocol of the future will regulate not only relations between human beings and between states, but also relations between human and machine, institution and algorithm, and state and artificial intelligence.

### 1. From AI tools to AI agents

The current model is largely request-and-response: a person asks and a system answers. The emerging model is agentic: systems can plan, sequence actions, use tools, communicate with other systems and perform bounded tasks with decreasing human intervention.

AI PROTOCOL distinguishes three layers that must never be confused:

- advice;
- decision;
- execution.

Machine capability may expand across all three. Legitimate authority does not transfer automatically with capability.

**Rule:** increased agency requires increased visibility of mandate, scope, revocation and responsibility.

### 2. Human authority in high-consequence decisions

Healthcare, law, security, finance, education, public administration and diplomacy require a higher standard than convenience or model performance.

A consequential AI workflow must preserve:

- an identifiable human owner;
- a visible mandate;
- a defined scope;
- a meaningful override path;
- a Human Gate before irreversible or rights-affecting action;
- final institutional accountability.

### 3. The right to know when one is interacting with AI

As synthetic voices, faces, documents and agents become increasingly indistinguishable from human counterparts, individuals require a basic transparency protection.

**Candidate right:** every person should be able to know when they are interacting with an artificial or substantially AI-mediated agent in a context where that fact is material.

This is not a ban on automation. It is a requirement of procedural honesty.

### 4. The right to explanation, challenge and human review

No person should suffer a serious institutional consequence solely because "the algorithm decided".

Where a decision materially affects rights, access, status, safety or livelihood, the governance architecture should preserve:

- an understandable explanation of the basis of the decision;
- access to the relevant evidence and provenance where lawfully possible;
- a path to challenge the result;
- escalation to accountable human review.

### 5. Authenticity and verified digital provenance

In the deepfake era, the key question is no longer only whether information exists, but whether a voice, image, video, document or instruction genuinely originates from the claimed source.

AI PROTOCOL therefore elevates **verified digital provenance** into a core governance requirement.

For consequential communications, institutions should be able to reconstruct:

**origin -> transformation -> model or agent -> human or institutional release -> final output**

Authenticity is part of authority.

### 6. AI, work and the distribution of productivity

The mature public question is not simply whether AI will remove jobs. It is how institutions and societies distribute the gains, dependencies and transition costs created by combined human-machine productivity.

AI PROTOCOL does not prescribe one economic model. It establishes a governance principle:

> Productivity gains created by AI must not be treated as separate from the human, social and institutional consequences through which they are produced.

### 7. AI literacy as civic literacy

AI literacy becomes a foundational capability for citizens, officials, professionals and students.

A literate user should know how to:

- distinguish discovery from authority;
- question outputs;
- verify sources;
- understand uncertainty;
- recognise manipulation;
- protect sensitive data;
- know when human escalation is required.

The aim is not to make every citizen an engineer. The aim is to prevent technical opacity from becoming institutional dependency.

### 8. Concentration of AI power and digital sovereignty

Advanced AI capabilities may become concentrated in a small number of companies, infrastructures or states.

AI PROTOCOL supports a balanced principle:

**AI sovereignty without AI isolation.**

Institutions and states should preserve sufficient control over data, mandate, continuity, verification and critical infrastructure without closing themselves off from international knowledge, standards or cooperation.

### 9. Intergenerational responsibility

AI governance is not only a present-day compliance matter.

The infrastructures, data practices, automated norms and institutional dependencies established today may shape the choices available to people in 2035, 2040 and beyond.

**Intergenerational principle:** a system should not be considered responsible merely because its immediate output is efficient; its long-term effect on human autonomy, institutional legitimacy and future choice must also be considered.

### 10. From reactive to anticipatory governance

Traditional regulation often responds after harm or conflict has already appeared. High-velocity AI systems require a more anticipatory sequence:

**anticipate -> test -> bound -> authorise -> deploy -> monitor -> correct -> learn**

This sequence complements the AI PROTOCOL Human Gate and the Institutional AI Cycle.

## The Right to Human Presence

AI PROTOCOL introduces the **Right to Human Presence** as a future-facing institutional safeguard.

Where an automated or AI-mediated process may significantly affect a person's rights, safety, legal position, health, access to essential services or institutional status, that person should have a meaningful route to accountable human consideration.

> No technological efficiency should completely extinguish the right of a human being to be heard by a human being.

The right is not absolute in every low-risk interaction. Its strength should rise with consequence.

### Human Presence trigger test

Human review should become mandatory or presumptively available when one or more of the following is present:

1. irreversible consequence;
2. material impact on rights or status;
3. credible dispute about facts;
4. model uncertainty or conflicting evidence;
5. vulnerability of the affected person;
6. absence of transparent reasoning;
7. institutional or legal requirement for accountable discretion.

## WPA 2040 Test

Before a major AI-enabled institutional policy, system or workflow is approved, decision-makers should ask:

1. **Human dignity** — does the system preserve the dignity of the person affected?
2. **Challenge** — can the person meaningfully contest the result?
3. **Responsibility** — is it clear who remains accountable?
4. **Traceability** — can the decision and source chain be reconstructed?
5. **Future acceptability** — would we accept the same rule if it governed our own children or successors in 2040?

A negative answer does not automatically prohibit deployment. It creates a presumption that the design is incomplete and requires additional safeguards, narrower scope or human review.

---

## Closing doctrine

AI may become faster, broader and more capable than the institutions that first deploy it. That does not make AI the holder of institutional legitimacy.

The central task of AI PROTOCOL is therefore not to slow intelligence, but to keep authority visible, dignity protected, responsibility attributable and correction possible.

> Capability may become machine-superior; legitimacy, rights and final institutional responsibility must remain human.
