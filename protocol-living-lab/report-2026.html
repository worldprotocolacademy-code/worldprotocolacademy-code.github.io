1 · What the evidence supports

Dated events

Six UK state visits are listed by the Royal Household between 2022 and 2025. They establish occurrence/date and public guest identity only; they do not establish that any imagined errors or dilemmas occurred.

Institutional procedures

UN Protocol Manual: credentials, mission registration and state-name/flag notifications. They illustrate UN procedural practice; the manual is not a universal protocol code.

Contextual normative anchors

Vienna Convention article 16 on precedence within relevant mission classes and IOC ceremony guidance on opening and parade order. These have different scopes and must not be conflated.

3 · Proposed research questions (NOT findings)

These questions guide future research rather than assert results. How do institutions document precedence changes? What types of source gaps recur across jurisdictions? Which decisions benefit from traceable human approval? How can teaching simulations preserve cultural context and confidentiality? Do different raters agree on a Protocolometry rubric under blinded review?

We do not presently publish answers, rates, correlations or league tables for these questions.

4 · Protocolometry and transparent scoring

The interactive simulator offers a bounded four-dimensional teaching rubric: applicable norms, evidence, coordination and Human Gate. Three multiple-choice steps per scenario are scored with pre-authored values 0–4; equal 25% weights. Missing answers are not scored. All choices and source links are inspectable. The methodology is a candidate, not a psychometrically validated assessment standard. Full standard →

5 · Known limitations and bias

  • Geographic bias: State-visit examples all come from the UK Royal Household chronology, not a representative world sample.
  • Source concentration: Only four source families are used; one Royal Household list supports six events.
  • Publication bias: Publicly documented protocol activities may omit nonpublic, less visible or multilingual practices.
  • Methodological bias: Rubric weights and answer values are authorial and unvalidated.
  • Selection bias: This curriculum was designed for educational clarity, not random or stratified scientific sampling.
  • Temporal limitation: Source content can be revised; archived evidence and retrieval dates need regular re-check.
  • No external validation: Independent review, reliability testing, sensitivity testing and real learner pilot remain outstanding.

7 · Review roadmap and release gates

Gate A · Corpus broadening

Increase geographic, linguistic and institutional diversity; record inclusion criteria, missing cases and source quality; conduct independent record-level fact-checks.

Gate B · Reliability pilot

Test simulations with consenting volunteers, accessibility checks and anonymous aggregate data only where a lawful collection framework is approved. Compare independent raters and publish methodology limits.

Gate C · External review

Obtain an actual independent methodological review before claiming a scientific instrument, comparative indicators or formal publication readiness.

Human Gate: The author/editor retains responsibility for version and publication; reviewers do not endorse WPA merely by being invited or cited. No badge issuance, public registry, automatic certification or commercial activation occurs in this prototype.
