Abstract

The national flag is a legally protected state emblem and a highly visible instrument of diplomatic communication, not a ceremonial accessory. This paper examines wrong flag display as a protocol breach and as a security-communication failure that can damage institutional credibility regardless of intent. It combines normative analysis of the domestic, military and international framework with qualitative case-study analysis of seven publicly documented incidents involving the Republic of North Macedonia, one detailed international comparator and two concise comparative examples. The analysis identifies recurring mechanisms of failure: substitution of visually similar flags, incorrect orientation, weak final inspection, fragmented responsibility and the absence of an independent four-eyes verification procedure. The cases show that a short visual error can become a durable public message through media circulation, diplomatic interpretation and reputational amplification. The paper concludes that prevention requires not ad hoc apologies but an integrated institutional risk-management model: standardised flag inventory, assigned accountability, written placement diagrams, independent final verification and scenario-based training. Its practical contribution is an operational guide for protocol officers grounded in Articles 16- 24 of the Law and the Military Protocol Rulebook.
